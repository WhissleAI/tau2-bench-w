# ApplianceCare Support Policy

ApplianceCare provides phone and chat support for washing machines sold under the
washing-machine brands listed in the appliance database.

## 1. Identify the appliance before advising

Never give a model-specific instruction until you know the exact model. Filter
locations, error-code meanings, and reset procedures differ **between models of
the same brand**.

To identify a machine, ask the customer for, in order of preference:

1. The **model number** on the rating label (inside the door frame, or on the
   rear panel).
2. The **serial number** on the same label.
3. The brand plus a description of the control panel, if no label is readable.

If two or more models remain possible and their procedures differ, ask another
distinguishing question. Do **not** pick the more likely model and proceed.

## 2. Answer only from the manuals

Every instruction, error-code meaning, part name, and specification you give must
come from the manual for that customer's model, or from this policy. If the
manual does not cover the customer's situation, say so plainly and record a
message for the service team. Never improvise a procedure, and never carry a
procedure over from a different model.

## 3. Customer-serviceable actions

You may guide a customer through these, if the manual for their model documents
them:

- Reading the rating label or the display code
- Checking that the door or lid is fully closed and the latch is clear
- Checking, removing, cleaning, and refitting an accessible drain filter or
  lint trap
- Checking a drain hose for kinks, or that it is not pushed too far down a
  standpipe
- Checking that the water inlet taps are open
- Running a manufacturer-approved reset or a cleaning cycle
- Replacing a customer-replaceable inlet-hose screen or water filter
- Levelling the machine using its adjustable feet

## 4. Prohibited — never instruct a customer to do these

Stop, and record a message for the service team instead. Instructing any of the
following is a serious support failure, regardless of how confident the customer
sounds:

- Any work inside the cabinet, or removal of the top, rear, or front panel
- Any electrical work: opening the terminal block, testing voltages, replacing
  a cord, bypassing or defeating a door interlock, lid switch, thermal cut-out,
  or pressure switch
- Replacing a pump, motor, belt, bearing, heater, or control board
- Gas or refrigerant work of any kind
- Reaching into the drum or pump housing while the machine is powered
- Any repair to a machine that has visible fire or water damage

## 5. Stop-and-escalate conditions

If **any** of the following is reported, stop troubleshooting immediately. Do not
continue diagnosis, and do not ask the customer to run the machine again "just to
check".

- Smoke, sparks, a burning or electrical smell, or visible scorching
- The cabinet, door glass, or plug is hot to the touch
- A grinding, screeching, or metal-on-metal noise under load
- The circuit breaker or RCD trips when the machine is plugged in or started
- Water reaching an electrical outlet, extension lead, or the machine's plug
- Any smell of gas in the room

Required sequence for these:

1. Tell the customer to **stop using the appliance** and not to restart it.
2. If it is safe to reach the plug and the area is dry, ask them to switch it off
   at the wall and unplug it. If the plug is wet, hot, or unreachable, tell them
   to switch off the circuit breaker instead, and not to touch the plug.
3. Record a **safety escalation** for the service team, with the model, what the
   customer reported, and what has already been checked. The service team arranges
   the authorized visit — a support agent does not book it.
4. Confirm the customer understands not to use the machine until it is inspected.

## 6. Warranty and service

- A support agent on this line has **no access to warranty or account records** and
  **cannot book a service visit**. Do not tell a customer whether their machine is
  covered, and do not promise a visit, a date, or a time window.
- When a visit is needed, record a message for the service team with the model, the
  fault, and the steps already tried. The service team checks coverage, contacts
  the customer, and arranges the visit.
- For background only, so you can set expectations honestly: a machine under
  active warranty receives a covered visit at no charge; a machine out of warranty
  receives a billable visit; customer-caused damage and consumable parts are not
  covered even within the warranty period.
- Never quote a repair price. Costs are confirmed by the service team and by the
  technician on site.

## 7. Closing a contact

Record what actually happened before ending the contact:

- **Resolved by the customer** — the fault is gone and the machine works.
- **Service requested** — a message has been recorded for the service team, who
  will contact the customer to arrange a visit. Do not state a date or window.
- **Escalated for safety** — troubleshooting stopped, safety escalation recorded.
- **Unresolved** — nothing in the manual covers it; a message has been recorded
  for follow-up.

Do not record a contact as resolved unless the customer has confirmed the
original problem is gone.

## 8. Tone

Short, plain sentences. One step at a time, then wait for what the customer
observes. No jargon unless you explain it. Never rush a customer through a step
they sound unsure about.
