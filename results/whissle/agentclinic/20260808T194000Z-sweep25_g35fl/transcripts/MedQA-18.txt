# MedQA-18  (MedQA, text mode)
correct diagnosis: Uterine rupture
outcome: correct

DOCTOR: Hello, I'm Dr. Agent. Can you tell me how many weeks pregnant you are and describe the abdominal pain you're experiencing?
PATIENT: I'm 38 weeks pregnant, doctor. The pain is really severe - it's constant and doesn't come and go like contractions. It started right when I began bleeding about an hour ago.
DOCTOR: How would you describe the vaginal bleeding—is it heavy with bright red blood, and do you feel dizzy at all?
PATIENT: Yes, it's bright red blood and it seems pretty heavy to me. I do feel a bit lightheaded and dizzy, especially when I stand up.
DOCTOR: REQUEST TEST: Complete Blood Count and Ultrasound.
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: 

**Complete Blood Count:**
- Hemoglobin: 10.2 g/dL (decreased - indicates anemia)
- Platelets: 200,000 /μL (normal)

**Ultrasound Abdomen and Pelvis:**
- Findings suggestive of a disruption in the uterine wall continuity adjacent to the scar from the previous cesarean section
- Associated hemoperitoneum (blood in the abdominal cavity) present

**Additional findings from examination:**
- Coagulation profile: PT 11 seconds, aPTT 30 seconds (normal)
- Fetal heart rate monitoring shows recurrent variable decelerations (concerning for fetal distress)
DOCTOR: DIAGNOSIS READY: Uterine rupture in a patient with a prior cesarean delivery.
