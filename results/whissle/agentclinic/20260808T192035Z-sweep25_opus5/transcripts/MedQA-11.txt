DOCTOR: Hello, I'm Dr. Agent — I'd like to understand these episodes better. Can you tell me how long each episode lasts and how often they happen?
PATIENT: Each episode lasts about 3 to 4 minutes, and they've been happening pretty often over the past month. I'm not really sure exactly how many times, but it feels like it's been several times.
DOCTOR: During an episode, do you have any warning beforehand — like a strange smell, taste, or a rising feeling in your stomach?
PATIENT: Yes, actually! Right before an episode starts, I get this weird muddy taste in my mouth. It's really strange and I don't like it.
DOCTOR: That taste is a helpful clue. After an episode ends, are you confused, sleepy, or unable to remember what happened for a while?
PATIENT: Yes, I feel really tired and confused afterwards. I don't remember anything that happened during the episode at all.
DOCTOR: Doctor: That pattern — an aura, loss of awareness, and confusion afterward — points to focal seizures. Has anyone witnessed you doing repetitive movements like lip-smacking, chewing, or picking at your clothes during these spells?
PATIENT: My brother saw me making hand gestures during one of them, like moving my hands around. I don't remember doing it though.
DOCTOR: That fits automatisms — a hallmark of temporal lobe seizures. Have you ever had a head injury, meningitis, or prolonged febrile seizures as a child?
PATIENT: No, I haven't had anything like that. I've been pretty healthy and never had any seizures before this started happening last month.
DOCTOR: Thank you — that's helpful. Have you noticed any fever, headache, or changes in your memory, mood, or behavior over the past month that your family has commented on?
PATIENT: No, I haven't had any fever or headaches. My parents haven't said anything about my memory or mood being different either—I feel pretty normal except for when these episodes happen.
DOCTOR: Let's get some imaging and brain wave testing to localize where these seizures are coming from.

REQUEST TEST: MRI_Brain

REQUEST TEST: EEG
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: 

**MRI Brain:**
No evidence of mass lesions, infarctions, or structural abnormalities.

**Electroencephalogram (EEG):**
Interictal spikes and sharp waves localized to the temporal lobes, more prominent on the right side.
DOCTOR: Your MRI is normal, but the EEG shows spike activity coming from the right temporal lobe — which matches your aura, automatisms, and post-episode confusion exactly.

DIAGNOSIS READY: Temporal lobe epilepsy — focal (right temporal) seizures with impaired awareness, MRI-negative.
