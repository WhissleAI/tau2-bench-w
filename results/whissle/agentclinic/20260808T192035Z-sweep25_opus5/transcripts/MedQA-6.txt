# MedQA-6  (MedQA, text mode)
correct diagnosis: Situational syncope

DOCTOR: Hello, I'm Dr. Agent — I understand you had an episode where you lost consciousness. Can you tell me what you were doing right before it happened?
PATIENT: I had just finished using the bathroom — you know, urinating — and then the next thing I knew, I was waking up on the floor with my wife standing over me looking worried.
DOCTOR: Did you have any warning before you went down — lightheadedness, nausea, sweating, or blurred vision?
PATIENT: No, not really — it just happened so suddenly. One moment I was standing there finishing up, and the next I was on the floor.
DOCTOR: How long were you out, and did your wife notice any jerking movements, tongue biting, or loss of bladder control — and were you confused when you came to?
PATIENT: My wife said it was only about 30 seconds, and no, I didn't bite my tongue or wet myself or anything like that. When I woke up, I knew exactly where I was and what had happened — no confusion at all.
DOCTOR: That fits with a faint rather than a seizure. Has this happened before, and were you getting up at night or had you been drinking alcohol beforehand?
PATIENT: No, this has never happened to me before — it really scared both of us. And no, I hadn't been drinking; this was just a regular trip to the bathroom during the day.
DOCTOR: Any medications you take — especially blood pressure pills or something for the prostate — and do you have any heart problems?
PATIENT: No, I don't take any medications at all — never really needed to. And I don't have any heart problems that I know of; I've always been pretty healthy overall.
DOCTOR: Any palpitations, chest pain, or shortness of breath just before or after the episode?
PATIENT: No, nothing like that — my chest felt fine the whole time. No racing heart, no pain, no trouble breathing before or after.
DOCTOR: Have you had any recent illness, poor fluid intake, or trouble with urinary flow — straining, hesitancy, or getting up multiple times at night?
PATIENT: No recent illness or anything like that, and I drink plenty of water throughout the day. As for urination, everything's been normal — no straining or trouble starting, and I only get up maybe once a night, if that.
DOCTOR: Let me check a couple of things — REQUEST TEST: Orthostatic_Blood_Pressure and REQUEST TEST: ECG_12_lead. While those run, may I ask your age and whether anyone in your family has had sudden cardiac death or fainting spells?

DOCTOR: Both tests are normal, which is reassuring. I didn't catch your age or family history — any relatives with sudden cardiac death or fainting spells?
PATIENT: I'm 53, and no, nothing like that in my family that I'm aware of. My parents are both still alive and doing well, and I don't have any siblings with heart problems or fainting issues.
DOCTOR: Thank you — that's reassuring. One last thing: were you standing the whole time you urinated, and have you had any headache, weakness, or numbness since?
PATIENT: Yes, I was standing the whole time — just like usual. And no, I haven't had any headache, weakness, or numbness since waking up; I actually feel completely normal now, which is why this whole thing is so strange to me.
DOCTOR: That all fits together — a brief faint while standing to urinate, with rapid full recovery, a normal ECG and no orthostatic drop, is the classic picture of a reflex (vasovagal) faint triggered by voiding.

DIAGNOSIS READY: Micturition syncope (situational reflex/vasovagal syncope) — I'd advise sitting to urinate at night, staying well hydrated, and rising slowly; return promptly if you have palpitations, chest pain, or a faint without warning while exerting yourself.
