PATIENT: No, it's not painful at all, which I found a bit odd. I haven't noticed any skin changes or anything coming from my nipple, and I don't think there's any swelling in my armpit either.

PATIENT: It feels pretty firm and smooth, and yes, it does seem to move around a bit when I touch it — it's not stuck in one place or anything like that.
DOCTOR: About how large would you say it is now, and did it seem to grow slowly at first or rapidly over just the past few weeks?
PATIENT: It's probably about the size of a golf ball now, maybe a little smaller. And it's grown rapidly — like I said, I only noticed it four weeks ago and it's already this big, which really worries me.
DOCTOR: Thank you — that rapid growth in a mobile, well-defined mass is important. Let's get imaging: REQUEST TEST: Bilateral_Diagnostic_Mammogram_and_Breast_Ultrasound
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: 

**Mammography Findings:**
- Smooth polylobulated mass identified in the left breast

**Biopsy Results:**

**Note:** These findings are consistent with the palpable 6-cm multinodular mass in the upper outer quadrant of the left breast. No axillary or cervical lymphadenopathy detected on examination.
DOCTOR: The imaging and biopsy show a smooth, polylobulated mass with leaf-like architecture and stromal overgrowth — that's a characteristic pattern, and it explains the rapid growth of a mobile, painless lump. The good news is there's no lymph node involvement.

DIAGNOSIS READY: Phyllodes tumor (cystosarcoma phyllodes) of the left breast — likely borderline/malignant given the stromal overgrowth and rapid enlargement; treatment is wide local excision with clear margins, and I'll refer you to breast surgery.
