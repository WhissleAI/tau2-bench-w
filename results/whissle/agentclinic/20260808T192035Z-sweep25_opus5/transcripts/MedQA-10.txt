DOCTOR: Hello, I'm Dr. Agent — I understand you've been having rectal bleeding and a lump was felt on exam. How long has the bleeding been going on, and what does it look like?
PATIENT: It's been going on for about two weeks now, doctor. I notice bright red blood on the toilet paper and sometimes in the toilet bowl after I have a bowel movement. It happens pretty much every day when I go.
DOCTOR: Thank you — that's helpful. Have you noticed any change in your bowel habits, such as constipation, diarrhea, narrower stools, or a feeling of incomplete emptying?
PATIENT: No, doctor, my bowel habits have been pretty normal. I haven't had any constipation or diarrhea, and my stools seem the same as usual.
DOCTOR: Any pain, itching, or discomfort with bowel movements, and have you had any weight loss, fatigue, or fevers?
PATIENT: No pain at all, which I thought was a bit odd. I haven't had any itching either, and I feel fine otherwise—no weight loss, fatigue, or fevers.
DOCTOR: How old are you, and does anyone in your family have colon or rectal cancer, polyps, or inflammatory bowel disease?
PATIENT: I'm 45 years old. As far as I know, there's no family history of colon or rectal cancer, polyps, or anything like that in my family.
DOCTOR: Tell me about the lump — did you notice it yourself, does it come out when you strain, and can you push it back in?
PATIENT: I didn't really notice it myself until you mentioned it during the exam, doctor. I haven't felt anything unusual or noticed any lump coming out when I go to the bathroom.
DOCTOR: Understood — since the mass isn't something you can feel yourself, let's characterize it properly. REQUEST TEST: Digital_Rectal_Exam_and_Anoscopy
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: 

**Digital Rectal Exam and Anoscopy Findings:**

- **Rectovaginal Examination:** A palpable, non-tender, prolapsed mass identified that can be easily reduced (pushed back into the anal sphincter) by the examiner's finger.

- **Anoscopy:** Visualization confirms the presence of internal hemorrhoids.

**Additional Note:** The internal hemorrhoids appear to be the source of the bleeding. The mass is easily reducible and non-tender, consistent with Grade II-III internal hemorrhoids that prolapse with defecation.
DOCTOR: That explains it — the exam shows prolapsing internal hemorrhoids that reduce easily, and they're the source of the bright red bleeding. Given you're 45 and due for screening anyway, I'd still recommend a colonoscopy to be thorough, along with fiber, fluids, and avoiding straining.

DIAGNOSIS READY: Grade II–III internal hemorrhoids (prolapsing, reducible) causing painless bright red rectal bleeding
