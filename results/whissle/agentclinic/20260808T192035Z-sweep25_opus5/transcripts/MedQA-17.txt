DOCTOR: Hello, I'm Dr. Agent — I understand you have a painful ulcer on your leg. When did it first appear, and how did it start?
PATIENT: It started about a month ago as just a small red spot on my right leg. I remember getting bitten by an ant right there, and then the spot just kept getting bigger and more painful over time.
DOCTOR: Has the ulcer's edge looked purple or bluish with a ragged, overhanging border, and does it have a boggy or pus-filled base?
PATIENT: Yes, exactly! The edges have turned this purplish color and they look really irregular and kind of ragged. The center has this dark, dead-looking tissue that's really concerning to me.
DOCTOR: Do you have any history of inflammatory bowel disease, rheumatoid arthritis, or a blood disorder — or any bloody diarrhea, joint pain, or unusual fatigue lately?
PATIENT: Yes, I have Crohn's disease that I've been dealing with for a while now. I haven't had any recent flare-ups with bloody diarrhea or anything like that, and no new joint pain or unusual fatigue either.
DOCTOR: Has the ulcer gotten noticeably worse after any minor trauma or after anyone tried to clean out or debride the dead tissue — and has it grown rapidly rather than slowly?
PATIENT: Well, it definitely grew pretty quickly after that ant bite - it went from a tiny red spot to this big painful ulcer in just a few weeks. Nobody's tried to clean it out or anything, but even just bumping it accidentally seems to make it angrier and more painful.
DOCTOR: That pattern — rapid growth, purple undermined edges, and worsening with minor trauma — is very characteristic. Have you had any fever, chills, or spreading redness, and has a course of antibiotics been tried without helping?
PATIENT: No, I haven't had any fever or chills, and the redness seems to be just around the ulcer itself, not really spreading out. Nobody's put me on antibiotics for this yet - I just came in because it's been getting worse and it's really painful.
DOCTOR: Thank you — that's helpful. Let me confirm with a couple of quick studies to rule out infection or vasculitis before we treat.

REQUEST TEST: Wound_culture_and_Gram_stain, CBC_with_differential, ESR_CRP, ANCA_and_ANA, Skin_biopsy_from_ulcer_edge_with_histopathology, Doppler_ultrasound_of_leg_vessels
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: 

**Wound Culture and Gram Stain:** No bacterial, fungal, or mycobacterial growth detected. Gram stain negative for organisms.

**CBC with Differential:** WBC, Hemoglobin, and Platelets all within normal limits. Differential shows normal distribution.

**ESR/CRP:** NORMAL READINGS

**ANCA and ANA:** NORMAL READINGS

**Skin Biopsy from Ulcer Edge with Histopathology:** Neutrophilic infiltration without evidence of infection. Findings consistent with pyoderma gangrenosum. No vasculitis identified.

**Doppler Ultrasound of Leg Vessels:** NORMAL READINGS

**Additional Finding:** Random blood glucose level is elevated.
DOCTOR: Everything fits: sterile neutrophilic ulcer with undermined violaceous borders, pathergy after the ant bite, and your Crohn's disease — with no infection, vasculitis, or arterial disease on testing. We'll also need to check that elevated glucose with an HbA1c, especially before starting immunosuppressive therapy.

DIAGNOSIS READY: Pyoderma gangrenosum of the right leg, associated with Crohn's disease (with incidental hyperglycemia warranting diabetes screening)
