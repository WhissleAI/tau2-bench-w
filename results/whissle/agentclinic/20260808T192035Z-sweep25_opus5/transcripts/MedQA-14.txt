# MedQA-14  (MedQA, text mode)
correct diagnosis: Rupture of the flexor digitorum profundus tendon at its point of insertion
outcome: correct

DOCTOR: Hello, I'm Dr. Agent — can you tell me when the swelling and pain in your right ring finger first started, and whether anything happened to it beforehand?
PATIENT: It started about 2 days ago when I was playing football. My finger got caught in another player's jersey, and he pulled away really hard. It's been swelling and hurting ever since.
DOCTOR: Can you bend the very tip of that ring finger down on its own — for example, curling just the fingertip while I hold the middle joint straight?
PATIENT: No, I can't bend the tip at all when you hold it like that. It just stays straight and it hurts when I try to force it.
DOCTOR: Where exactly is the pain and tenderness worst — right at the fingertip, or does it extend down into your palm?
PATIENT: The pain is worst right at the joint closest to my fingertip, not really down in my palm. It's pretty tender right there when I touch it or try to move it.
DOCTOR: Is there any bruising, or can you feel a tender lump anywhere along the front of the finger or in your palm where the tendon may have pulled back?
PATIENT: There's some bruising around the joint, but I don't really feel any lump in my palm. The tenderness is mostly just right at that joint near the tip of my finger.
DOCTOR: That's a classic "jersey finger" pattern — let me confirm whether a piece of bone was pulled off with the tendon.

REQUEST TEST: X-Ray_Right_Ring_Finger (PA and lateral views)
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: X-ray of the Right Hand shows no bone fractures or dislocations visible. Soft tissue swelling noted around the distal interphalangeal joint of the right ring finger.
DOCTOR: No fracture — so the tendon itself has torn away. Let me localize how far it has pulled back.

REQUEST TEST: Ultrasound_Right_Ring_Finger_Flexor_Tendon
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: The requested Ultrasound_Right_Ring_Finger_Flexor_Tendon is not available in the current data. However, based on the available MRI findings:

MRI of the Right Hand (Preliminary Findings) is expected to show details regarding the integrity of tendons.

**Note:** An ultrasound of the flexor tendon would typically show:
- Tendon discontinuity
- Retraction level of the torn tendon end
- Fluid collection in the tendon sheath
- Absence of normal tendon structure at the insertion site

For definitive assessment of flexor tendon integrity and retraction level in this case, the MRI results (when complete) or direct ultrasound imaging would be recommended.
DOCTOR: Let's get the MRI, then — it will show exactly where the tendon end has retracted to.

REQUEST TEST: MRI_Right_Hand (flexor tendon protocol)
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: MRI of the Right Hand shows complete rupture of the flexor digitorum profundus (FDP) tendon of the right ring finger at its insertion on the distal phalanx. The tendon has retracted proximally to the level of the proximal interphalangeal (PIP) joint. Moderate surrounding soft tissue edema and fluid within the flexor tendon sheath are noted. No bony avulsion fragment identified. These findings are consistent with a Zone 1 flexor tendon injury (jersey finger).
DOCTOR: That confirms it: the deep flexor tendon has torn off the bone and pulled back to the middle joint, with no bone fragment attached — this needs surgical repair within about a week, so I'll refer you to a hand surgeon urgently and splint the finger in the meantime.

DIAGNOSIS READY: Jersey finger — acute Zone 1 complete rupture (avulsion) of the flexor digitorum profundus tendon of the right ring finger with retraction to the PIP joint (Leddy–Packer Type II, no bony avulsion).
