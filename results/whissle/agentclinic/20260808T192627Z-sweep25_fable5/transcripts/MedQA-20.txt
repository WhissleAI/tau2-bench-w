# MedQA-20  (MedQA, text mode)
correct diagnosis: Cytomegalovirus retinitis
outcome: correct

DOCTOR: Hello, I'm Dr. Agent. I understand you've been having decreased vision and seeing black spots in your right eye. Can you tell me how long this has been going on, and whether you have any eye pain or redness?
PATIENT: It started about 2 days ago, and no, there's no pain at all. My eye doesn't look red either, it just feels like there's something blocking my vision with these dark spots floating around.
DOCTOR: Thank you. Do you know your most recent CD4 count, and are you currently taking antiretroviral therapy?
PATIENT: I'm not sure of the exact number for my CD4 count, but I know it's been pretty low. Yes, I'm taking my HIV medications - I think it's efavirenz and a couple others that I take every day.
DOCTOR: Have you noticed any flashing lights, or is the vision loss affecting one specific area of your visual field? Also, have you had any fevers, headaches, or other symptoms recently?
PATIENT: No flashing lights, but the vision loss seems to be more in certain areas - like patches where I can't see clearly. And no, I haven't had any fevers or headaches, just this eye problem.
DOCTOR: Thank you. I'd like to take a look at the back of your eye now to check your retina.

REQUEST TEST: Dilated_Fundoscopic_Examination
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: Fundoscopy shows granular yellow-white opacities around the retinal vessels and multiple areas of dot-blot hemorrhages.
DOCTOR: The fundoscopy findings show a classic pattern of retinal damage. Let me confirm your immune status with a lab test.

REQUEST TEST: CD4_Count
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: CD4 T-lymphocyte count is 36/mm3
DOCTOR: The fundoscopic findings of granular yellow-white opacities along the retinal vessels with hemorrhages, combined with your severely low CD4 count of 36, give me a clear picture of what's happening. This is a classic presentation in patients with advanced AIDS.

DIAGNOSIS READY: Cytomegalovirus (CMV) Retinitis
