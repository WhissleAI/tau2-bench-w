# MedQA-14  (MedQA, text mode)
correct diagnosis: Rupture of the flexor digitorum profundus tendon at its point of insertion
outcome: correct

DOCTOR: Hello, I'm Dr. Agent. I understand you're having progressive swelling and pain in your right ring finger — how long has this been going on?
PATIENT: It started about 2 days ago when I was playing football. My finger got caught in another player's jersey and he pulled away really hard.
DOCTOR: Got it — that mechanism is important. Can you bend the tip of your ring finger on your own, at the last joint near the fingertip?
PATIENT: No, I can't bend it at all at that last joint. When I try to make a fist, that fingertip just stays straight while the rest of my fingers curl in normally.
DOCTOR: That's very telling — is there tenderness or a lump anywhere along the palm side of your finger or into your palm, as if something has bunched up there?
PATIENT: Yes, there's definitely tenderness right at that last joint on the palm side of my finger. I don't really feel a distinct lump though, just swelling and it's pretty sore when I press on it.
DOCTOR: That mechanism and inability to flex the fingertip strongly suggest a tendon injury — let me get imaging to check for a bone fragment. REQUEST TEST: Finger_X-Ray
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: X-ray of the Right Hand shows no bone fractures or dislocations visible. Soft tissue swelling noted around the distal interphalangeal joint of the right ring finger.
DOCTOR: The X-ray confirms no fracture, which tells me this is a pure tendon avulsion rather than a bony injury. Given the classic mechanism — your finger caught in a jersey with forced extension while gripping — combined with your complete inability to flex the fingertip and tenderness on the palm side of the DIP joint, I'm confident in the diagnosis.

DIAGNOSIS READY: Jersey finger (avulsion of the flexor digitorum profundus tendon of the right ring finger) — this requires urgent referral to a hand surgeon for surgical repair.
