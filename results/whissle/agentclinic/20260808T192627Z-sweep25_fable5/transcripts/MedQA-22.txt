DOCTOR: Hello, I'm Dr. Agent. I understand you have a skin lesion on your chest — can you tell me how long it's been there and whether it's changed in size or appearance?
PATIENT: Yes, Doctor. I first noticed it about two months ago, and I'm pretty sure it's gotten bigger since then. It doesn't hurt or itch or anything like that, though.
DOCTOR: Can you describe what the lesion looks like — its color, shape, size, and whether the borders are regular or irregular?
PATIENT: It's kind of dark brown, maybe a bit irregular in color — not all one shade. The edges aren't perfectly smooth, they're a bit jagged or uneven. I'd say it's about the size of a pencil eraser now, maybe a little bigger.
DOCTOR: Have you noticed any bleeding, crusting, or ulceration of the lesion, or does it ever ooze or scab over?
PATIENT: No, I haven't seen any bleeding or oozing from it. It hasn't formed any scabs or crusts either — it's just been sitting there getting bigger.
DOCTOR: Do you have a personal or family history of skin cancer, and have you had significant sun exposure or sunburns over the years?
PATIENT: I don't think anyone in my family has had skin cancer that I know of. As for sun exposure, well, I've spent a fair amount of time outdoors over the years, but I can't recall any really bad sunburns or anything like that.
DOCTOR: Do you have many other moles on your body, and have you noticed if this one looks different from the rest — the so-called "ugly duckling" sign?
PATIENT: I do have a few other moles here and there, but yeah, this one definitely looks different from the others. The other ones are smaller and more uniform in color, so this one really stands out compared to them.
DOCTOR: That's important information. Have you noticed any new lumps in your armpits or neck, or any unexplained weight loss, fatigue, or night sweats?
PATIENT: No, I haven't noticed any lumps in my armpits or neck. And I haven't had any weight loss, fatigue, or night sweats either — I've been feeling pretty normal otherwise.
DOCTOR: That's reassuring. Given the concerning features, I'd like to examine the lesion more closely with a dermatoscope.

REQUEST TEST: Dermoscopy
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: 

**Dermoscopy Findings:**

The dermoscopic examination reveals several concerning features consistent with the clinical presentation:

- **Asymmetry in structure and color distribution**
- **Irregular, atypical pigment network** with areas of network disruption
- **Multiple colors present** including dark brown, light brown, and areas of black pigmentation
- **Irregular dots and globules** distributed asymmetrically throughout the lesion
- **Atypical vascular patterns** noted
- **Blue-white veil** present in portions of the lesion
- **Irregular borders** with pseudopods and radial streaming at the periphery

**Dermoscopy Score:** High suspicion for melanoma based on ABCD rule and revised 7-point checklist

These dermoscopic features are highly suggestive of malignant melanoma and warrant immediate biopsy for histopathological confirmation.
DOCTOR: The dermoscopy shows several very concerning features, so I want to confirm this with a tissue sample under the microscope.

REQUEST TEST: Excisional_Skin_Biopsy
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS:

**Excisional Skin Biopsy - Histopathology Report**

**Specimen:** Excisional biopsy of chest lesion

**Histopathology Findings:**
Atypical melanocytes that infiltrate the epidermis and dermis, confirming the diagnosis of **malignant melanoma**.

**Microscopic Description:**
- Proliferation of atypical melanocytes with nuclear pleomorphism and hyperchromasia
- Melanocytes present as single cells and nests throughout the epidermis
- Invasion into the papillary and reticular dermis identified
- Increased mitotic activity observed
- Loss of normal maturation with descent into dermis

**Diagnosis:** **MALIGNANT MELANOMA**

**Additional Pathological Details:**
- Breslow thickness and Clark level assessment recommended for staging
- Margins evaluation pending
- Ulceration status to be determined
- Sentinel lymph node biopsy may be indicated based on final staging
