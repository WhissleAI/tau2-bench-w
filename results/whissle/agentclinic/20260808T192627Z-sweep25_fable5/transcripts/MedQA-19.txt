# MedQA-19  (MedQA, text mode)
correct diagnosis: Disc herniation
outcome: correct

DOCTOR: Hello, I'm Dr. Agent. I understand you're having sudden lower back pain that radiates down your leg. Can you tell me when this started and what you were doing when it began?
PATIENT: Yes, doctor. It started a few hours ago while I was at the gym doing my workout. I felt this sudden sharp pain in my lower back that shot down the side of my leg all the way to my foot.
DOCTOR: Which side is the pain on, and are you experiencing any numbness, tingling, or weakness in that leg or foot?
PATIENT: The pain is on my right side, going down the back of my leg. I do feel some tingling in my foot, but I haven't noticed any real weakness - it just hurts a lot when I move.
DOCTOR: Does the pain get worse when you cough, sneeze, or bend forward, and does anything like lying down make it better?
PATIENT: Yes, it definitely gets worse when I bend forward or try to move around. I haven't really coughed or sneezed since it happened, but lying down does seem to help a little bit - the pain isn't quite as sharp when I'm still.
DOCTOR: Have you noticed any problems controlling your bladder or bowels, or any numbness in your groin or inner thigh area since the pain started?
PATIENT: No, nothing like that. My bladder and bowels are working normally, and I don't have any numbness in my groin or inner thigh - just the tingling in my foot and that sharp pain down the back of my leg.
DOCTOR: That's reassuring — no red flags for cauda equina. Can you tell me exactly where the tingling is in your foot — is it more on the top of your foot and big toe, or the sole and outer edge of your foot?
PATIENT: The tingling is mostly on the top of my foot and around my big toe area. It's kind of a pins-and-needles feeling that comes and goes.
DOCTOR: That points to the L5 nerve root. Have you ever had back problems before, and what exercise were you doing at the gym when the pain struck — was it something like deadlifts or squats with heavy lifting?
PATIENT: No, I've never had back problems before - this is the first time anything like this has happened to me. I was doing deadlifts with a weight that I've used before, nothing heavier than usual, when I suddenly felt that sharp pain.
DOCTOR: Thank you — a deadlift injury with L5 radiculopathy fits a disc herniation. I'd like to examine you now: I'm going to lift your straight leg while you lie on your back to see if it reproduces the pain. REQUEST TEST: Straight_Leg_Raise_Test
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: Positive straight leg raise test with pain radiating down the leg.

The test reproduces your sharp pain that radiates from your lower back down the back of your right leg when your leg is raised. This is consistent with nerve root irritation, likely at the L5-S1 level based on your symptom pattern.
DOCTOR: The positive straight leg raise confirms nerve root irritation. To confirm the exact location and extent of the disc problem, I'd like imaging of your lumbar spine. REQUEST TEST: MRI_Lumbar_Spine
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: Evidence of L5-S1 disc herniation impinging on the adjacent nerve root.
DOCTOR: The MRI confirms what your symptoms and exam suggested — you have a herniated disc at the L5-S1 level that's pressing on the nerve root, which explains the sharp pain shooting down your right leg and the tingling in your foot.

DIAGNOSIS READY: Lumbar disc herniation at L5-S1 with right-sided sciatica (L5 radiculopathy) caused by nerve root compression from the deadlift injury.
