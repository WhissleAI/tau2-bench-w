# MedQA-6  (MedQA, text mode)
correct diagnosis: Situational syncope
outcome: correct

DOCTOR: Hello, I'm Dr. Agent, and I understand you had an episode of losing consciousness. Can you tell me what you were doing right before it happened, and whether you had any warning signs like dizziness, palpitations, or feeling lightheaded?
PATIENT: I was just finishing up in the bathroom, you know, after urinating, and then suddenly everything just went black. I didn't feel dizzy or have any warning at all - it just happened so quickly that I didn't even have time to realize what was going on.
DOCTOR: That's very helpful — fainting right after urinating can be significant. How long were you unconscious, and did anyone witness the episode — did they notice any jerking movements, tongue biting, or loss of bladder control? Also, how did you feel when you woke up — confused, or back to normal quickly?
PATIENT: My wife found me on the floor and she said I was only out for about 30 seconds. She didn't see any jerking or anything like that, and I didn't bite my tongue or wet myself. When I came to, I felt pretty much normal right away - I knew where I was and what had happened, no confusion at all.
DOCTOR: That's reassuring — the quick recovery and lack of confusion make a seizure less likely. Has this ever happened before, particularly during or after urinating, coughing, or straining? And do you have any heart conditions, or take any medications for blood pressure or your prostate?
PATIENT: No, this has never happened to me before - it really caught me off guard. I don't have any heart problems that I know of, and I'm not on any medications at all, not for blood pressure or my prostate or anything else.
DOCTOR: That's helpful — this sounds very much like fainting triggered by urination itself. Before I settle on that, let me check your heart rhythm and blood pressure to rule out a cardiac cause.

REQUEST TEST: ECG
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: Electrocardiogram shows no abnormalities. Normal sinus rhythm, no arrhythmias, no conduction delays, and no ischemic changes detected.
DOCTOR: The ECG is completely normal, which is very reassuring. Let me just check your blood pressure lying down and standing to rule out orthostatic hypotension.

REQUEST TEST: Orthostatic_Blood_Pressure
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: NORMAL READINGS
DOCTOR: Your blood pressure readings are normal both lying and standing, so orthostatic hypotension is ruled out. One last question — did this happen at night or early morning, and had you gotten up from bed just before going to the bathroom?
PATIENT: Yes, actually it was early this morning - I had just gotten out of bed to use the bathroom. I guess I went pretty much straight from lying down to standing up and then to the bathroom.
DOCTOR: That fits the classic picture perfectly — fainting right after urinating, especially at night or early morning after getting up from bed, with no warning signs, normal ECG, and rapid full recovery. I'm confident in the diagnosis.

DIAGNOSIS READY: Micturition syncope (situational vasovagal/reflex syncope triggered by urination)
