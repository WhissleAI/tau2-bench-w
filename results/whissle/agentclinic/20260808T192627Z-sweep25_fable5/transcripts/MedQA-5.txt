DOCTOR: Hello, I'm Dr. Agent. I understand you're having knee pain after increasing your physical activity recently. Can you tell me exactly where on your knee the pain is located — front, sides, or behind the kneecap?
PATIENT: The pain is on the inner side of my knee, just below my kneecap. It's been getting worse since yesterday after I played volleyball with my new team.
DOCTOR: Got it — inner side, just below the kneecap. Did you feel a pop, twist, or any specific injury during volleyball, or did the pain come on gradually afterward?
PATIENT: No, I didn't feel any pop or specific moment where I got injured. The pain just came on gradually and has been getting worse since then.
DOCTOR: Okay, gradual onset with no acute injury — that's helpful. Is there any swelling, redness, or warmth over that spot on the inner side of your knee?
PATIENT: No, I haven't noticed any swelling, redness, or warmth. It just hurts, especially when I move around or put weight on it.
DOCTOR: Does the pain get worse with specific movements like climbing stairs, squatting, or getting up from a seated position? And is that spot tender when you press on it directly?
PATIENT: Yes, it definitely hurts more when I climb stairs or get up from sitting. And yes, when I press on that spot directly, it's pretty tender and sore.
DOCTOR: Thank you — tenderness at the inner knee below the kneecap with pain on stairs is telling. Do you feel any locking, catching, or giving way of the knee, or is the pain worse at night or when your knees touch while lying on your side?
PATIENT: No, I haven't had any locking or catching, and my knee doesn't give way. The pain is mostly just when I'm active or putting weight on it, not really at night or when I'm lying down.
DOCTOR: Thank you — that makes serious internal injury less likely. Have you noticed any pain or tightness in your hamstrings or inner thigh muscles, and have you significantly increased your jumping or running lately with this new volleyball team?
PATIENT: Yes, actually my inner thigh muscles have felt pretty tight lately. And yes, I've definitely been doing a lot more jumping and running since I joined the volleyball team — it's way more activity than I'm used to.
DOCTOR: That tightness combined with the sudden jump in activity fits a clear pattern. Let me examine your knee to confirm the exact spot of tenderness and rule out ligament involvement.

DIAGNOSIS READY: Pes anserine bursitis (pes anserinus bursitis/tendinopathy) — an overuse inflammation of the bursa at the inner knee below the kneecap where the hamstring and inner thigh tendons attach, caused by the sudden increase in volleyball activity and tight hamstring/adductor muscles.
