DOCTOR: Is the swelling painful or tender to touch, and have you noticed any redness or warmth over the area?
PATIENT: No, it's not painful at all, and I haven't noticed any redness or warmth over it. It's just been growing slowly without bothering me much.
DOCTOR: Can you tell me if the lump feels soft and rubbery or hard, and does it move around under the skin when you press on it?
PATIENT: It feels kind of soft and rubbery, and yes, it does seem to move a bit when I press on it. It's not fixed in place or anything like that.
DOCTOR: Have you had any fevers, night sweats, unexplained weight loss, or fatigue over these past two months?
PATIENT: No, I haven't had any fevers, night sweats, or weight loss. I've been feeling pretty much like my usual self, just with this lump on my neck.
DOCTOR: Have you noticed any similar lumps elsewhere on your body, and does anyone in your family have a history of similar fatty lumps or growths?
PATIENT: No, I haven't noticed any other lumps anywhere else on my body. And as for my family, I'm not aware of anyone having similar fatty lumps or growths like this.
DOCTOR: Thank you. The lump sounds like a benign fatty growth, but I'd like to confirm with imaging before making a diagnosis.

PATIENT: No, I haven't noticed any dark spot or opening on the skin over it, and it's never leaked anything. It's just been a smooth swelling without any discharge.
DOCTOR: That's helpful — cysts without a punctum on the back of the neck are often trichilemmal or epidermoid cysts. To be thorough, I'd like a sample of the lesion analyzed.

However, based on the clinical presentation of a 2.5-cm firm, mobile, painless nodule on the nape of the neck with skin that cannot be pinched over it, and ultrasound showing a well-circumscribed cystic lesion in subcutaneous tissue, this is consistent with a benign cystic lesion. NORMAL READINGS for available blood work (CBC and serum chemistry show no concerning abnormalities aside from elevated glucose).
DOCTOR: The findings all point one way — a well-circumscribed subcutaneous cystic lesion on the nape of the neck, firm, mobile, painless, with skin tethered over it and no punctum. This is characteristic of a benign cyst of hair follicle origin, and given the location and absence of a punctum, a trichilemmal cyst is most likely.

DIAGNOSIS READY: Trichilemmal (pilar) cyst of the nape of the neck — a benign cystic lesion (differential: epidermoid cyst), treatable with simple surgical excision.
