DOCTOR: Hello, I'm Dr. Agent. Can you tell me when this leg lesion first appeared and how it started — did it begin as a small bump, a blister, or something else?
PATIENT: Hello, Doctor. It started about a month ago as a small red spot on my right leg. I remember getting an ant bite there right before it appeared, and then it just kept getting bigger and more painful over time.
DOCTOR: That's helpful — a lesion that grows after minor trauma is important. Can you describe the ulcer's appearance now — its size, the color of its edges, and whether there's any drainage or pus?
PATIENT: It's gotten quite large and really painful. The center looks kind of dark and dead, and the edges are purplish and irregular. I haven't noticed much drainage or pus coming from it.
DOCTOR: That description — a necrotic center with purplish, irregular, undermined-looking edges — is very telling. Do you have any other medical conditions, particularly inflammatory bowel disease like Crohn's or ulcerative colitis, arthritis, or any blood disorders?
PATIENT: Yes, I do have Crohn's disease, and I also have type 2 diabetes and high blood pressure. Oh, and about 8 months ago I was treated for some eye inflammation — they called it anterior uveitis.
DOCTOR: That history of Crohn's disease and uveitis fits with an immune-mediated process. Have you noticed if the ulcer gets worse or new lesions form after minor injuries, like the ant bite or any scrapes — a phenomenon called pathergy? And do you have any fever or chills?
PATIENT: I haven't had any fever or chills. As for the pathergy thing, I'm not sure — this is the only lesion I've had, and it did start right where that ant bite was, so maybe? I haven't had other injuries to compare it to.
DOCTOR: That's very helpful — the lesion starting exactly at the site of minor trauma does suggest pathergy. I'd like to rule out infection and confirm the diagnosis before we consider treatment.

MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: 

**Skin Biopsy with Tissue Culture:**

**Histopathology Findings:**
- Neutrophilic infiltration without infection, consistent with pyoderma gangrenosum

**Tissue Culture:**
- No bacterial growth

These results confirm pyoderma gangrenosum with no evidence of infectious etiology. The neutrophilic infiltration pattern and absence of microorganisms support a sterile inflammatory process rather than an infectious cause of the ulceration.
DOCTOR: DIAGNOSIS READY: Pyoderma gangrenosum — a sterile neutrophilic dermatosis associated with your Crohn's disease, confirmed by biopsy showing neutrophilic infiltration with negative tissue cultures, and consistent with the pathergy response at the ant bite site, the necrotic ulcer with violaceous undermined borders, and your history of uveitis.
