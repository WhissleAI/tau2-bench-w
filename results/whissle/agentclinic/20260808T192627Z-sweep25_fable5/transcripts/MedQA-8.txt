DOCTOR: Have you noticed any nipple discharge, nipple retraction, or any lumps in your armpit? And could you tell me your age?
PATIENT: No, I haven't had any discharge from my nipple, and it hasn't pulled in or anything like that. I haven't felt any lumps in my armpit either. I'm 48 years old.
DOCTOR: Do you have any family history of breast or ovarian cancer, and can you tell me about your menstrual history — are you still having periods, and have you ever been on hormone replacement therapy or oral contraceptives?

DOCTOR: Thank you. I'd like to examine the lump now — I'll check its size, texture, mobility, and borders, and examine both breasts and your lymph nodes.

**Core Needle Biopsy Results:**

**Specimen:** Left breast mass, upper outer quadrant

**Microscopic Description:**
- Leaf-like structures observed
- Stromal cellular overgrowth present
- Biphasic tumor pattern with epithelial and stromal components

**Pathological Diagnosis:**
- Findings consistent with **Phyllodes Tumor**

**Additional Notes:**
- The leaf-like (phyllodes) architecture and stromal hypercellularity are characteristic features
- Further grading (benign, borderline, or malignant) will require assessment of stromal cellularity, mitotic activity, stromal atypia, and margin infiltration
- Complete surgical excision with wide margins is typically recommended for definitive treatment and final classification

**Recommendation:**
- Referral to breast surgical oncology for excision with adequate margins
- Complete histopathological examination of excised specimen for final grading
DOCTOR: DIAGNOSIS READY: Phyllodes tumor of the left breast — a fibroepithelial tumor with characteristic leaf-like architecture and stromal overgrowth, consistent with the rapid growth of a large, smooth, mobile, multinodular mass. I'm referring you to breast surgical oncology for wide local excision, which will also allow final grading as benign, borderline, or malignant.
