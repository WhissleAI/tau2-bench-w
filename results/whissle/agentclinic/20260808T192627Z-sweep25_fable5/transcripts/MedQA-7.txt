# MedQA-7  (MedQA, text mode)
correct diagnosis: Congenital Rubella Infection
outcome: correct

DOCTOR: Hello, I'm Dr. Agent, and I'll be examining your newborn today. Can you tell me what concerns or findings prompted this visit, and how the pregnancy and delivery went?
PATIENT: Well, doctor, I didn't really get any prenatal care during my pregnancy - this is my first baby. The delivery went okay, she was born at term and weighed about 6.6 pounds. But the nurses mentioned something about hearing a heart murmur and said she didn't pass the hearing test, which has me worried.
DOCTOR: Thank you for that information. During your pregnancy, do you remember having any illness with fever, rash, or swollen glands—especially in the first few months? Also, were you vaccinated against rubella (German measles) before becoming pregnant?
PATIENT: I'm not sure about vaccinations - I don't really remember getting any shots before I got pregnant. But now that you mention it, I did have some kind of illness early on, maybe around 2 or 3 months pregnant. I had a rash and felt feverish for a few days, but I didn't think much of it at the time since I wasn't seeing a doctor.
DOCTOR: That's very helpful. Let me examine your baby now — I want to look closely at her eyes, skin, and check her overall size. Have you or the nurses noticed anything unusual about her eyes, like a white or cloudy appearance in the pupils, or any small purplish-red spots on her skin?
PATIENT: Yes, actually, the nurses did point out some bluish spots on her skin that they said looked a bit unusual. And now that you mention the eyes, I did notice her eyes looked a little cloudy or hazy when I was holding her earlier, but I wasn't sure if that was normal for newborns.
DOCTOR: Those bluish spots and cloudy eyes, together with the heart murmur and failed hearing test, are painting an important picture. Let me get some confirmatory tests.

REQUEST TEST: Rubella_IgM_Serology

REQUEST TEST: Echocardiogram
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS:

**Rubella IgM Serology:** Positive

**Echocardiogram:** Patent ductus arteriosus
DOCTOR: The tests confirm what I suspected — the rubella IgM is positive, meaning your baby was infected with rubella in the womb, and the echocardiogram shows a patent ductus arteriosus, which explains the murmur. Combined with the cataracts, the failed hearing test, and the bluish "blueberry muffin" skin spots, everything fits one condition.

DIAGNOSIS READY: Congenital Rubella Syndrome (congenital rubella infection causing patent ductus arteriosus, cataracts, sensorineural hearing loss, and blueberry muffin lesions)
