DOCTOR: Good morning, and thank you for coming in today. I understand you've been experiencing progressive swelling and pain in your right ring finger—let me start by asking: when did you first notice this swelling begin?
PATIENT: Yes, it started about 2 days ago when I was playing football and another player grabbed my jersey and pulled my finger away really hard. Since then, the swelling has gotten worse and it's been pretty painful.
DOCTOR: I see—so this was a traumatic injury from being grabbed during football. On a scale of 0 to 10, how would you rate the pain right now, and has the swelling been continuous since the injury or does it come and go?
PATIENT: I'd say the pain is about a 6 or 7 out of 10 right now, and the swelling has been pretty much continuous since it happened—it hasn't really gone down at all. Also, I've noticed I can't bend the tip of my finger when I try to make a fist, and my finger just stays kind of extended even when I'm resting it.
DOCTOR: That's important information—the inability to bend the tip of your finger suggests a potential tendon injury. Let me examine your hand more closely and then request some imaging. Can you tell me: is there any numbness, tingling, or color changes in the finger, and have you had any fever or signs of infection like warmth or redness around the injury?
PATIENT: No, I haven't had any fever or signs of infection—the area isn't particularly warm or red, just swollen and tender. And I haven't experienced any numbness or tingling either, just the pain and inability to bend my finger at the tip.
DOCTOR: Thank you—that's helpful. Before I examine you further, let me request an X-ray to check for any fractures or joint involvement, since this was a significant trauma.
