DOCTOR: Good morning, I'm Dr. Agent. I understand you've been having episodes of unresponsiveness with facial grimacing—that sounds concerning. Can you tell me when you first started experiencing these episodes?
PATIENT: Well, doctor, it started about a month ago, and they happen pretty often now. Before each one starts, my mouth gets a weird, muddy taste that kind of warns me something's about to happen.
DOCTOR: That's very helpful information—the warning taste is an important clue. How often are these episodes occurring now, and how long does each one typically last?
PATIENT: They happen pretty much every few days, doctor, and each one only lasts about 3 or 4 minutes. After they're over, I feel really tired and confused, and I don't remember anything that happened during the episode.
DOCTOR: Thank you for that detail. The amnesia after the episode is significant. During these episodes when you're unresponsive, are you aware of what's happening around you, or is it a complete blank?
PATIENT: It's a complete blank for me, doctor—I don't remember anything at all while it's happening. My brother said he saw me staring and making weird hand movements during one of them, but I have no idea I was doing that.
DOCTOR: That's very important—the repetitive hand movements combined with staring and post-event confusion are significant findings. Have you had any head injuries, high fevers, or noticed any triggers like stress, lack of sleep, or certain times of day when these episodes are more likely to happen?
PATIENT: I haven't had any head injuries or high fevers, doctor, and I don't think there are any particular times of day when they happen more. I'm not sure if stress or missing sleep makes them worse—I've just been having them come out of the blue.
DOCTOR: I understand. Let me ask about your medical history—do you have any ongoing conditions like diabetes, heart problems, or have you ever been diagnosed with epilepsy or seizures before?
PATIENT: No, doctor, I've never had seizures or any of those conditions before—this is all new to me. I'm usually pretty healthy and don't take any medicines or anything like that.
DOCTOR: That's helpful to know. I'd like to request an EEG to record your brain's electrical activity, as your symptoms—the warning taste, unresponsiveness, hand movements, and post-event confusion—are suggestive of seizure activity. REQUEST TEST: EEG
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: Electroencephalogram - Interictal spikes and sharp waves localized to the temporal lobes, more prominent on the right side.
DOCTOR: The EEG findings are quite telling—those temporal lobe spikes are consistent with focal seizures. Has anyone in your family ever had seizures, epilepsy, or any neurological conditions that you're aware of?
PATIENT: No, doctor, nobody in my family has ever had seizures or epilepsy that I know of. My parents and my brother are all healthy, and we don't have any family history of neurological problems.
DOCTOR: That's useful to know. I'd like to request an MRI of your brain to look for any structural abnormalities in the temporal lobe that might be causing these seizures.

REQUEST TEST: Brain_MRI
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: MRI Brain - No evidence of mass lesions, infarctions, or structural abnormalities.
DOCTOR: The MRI doesn't show any structural problems, which is reassuring. One more important question—have you noticed any other symptoms like headaches, vision changes, or any strange sensations before the episodes besides that muddy taste?
PATIENT: No, doctor, I don't have headaches or any vision problems. The only thing I notice before it happens is that muddy taste in my mouth—that's really the only warning sign I get.
DOCTOR: Thank you for that clarity. Given your EEG findings showing temporal lobe spikes, the recurrent episodes with complete amnesia, repetitive hand movements, post-event confusion, and that distinctive muddy taste as a warning—along with a normal brain MRI—I have enough information to make my assessment.

DIAGNOSIS READY: Temporal Lobe Epilepsy (TLE) with focal seizures originating from the right temporal lobe
