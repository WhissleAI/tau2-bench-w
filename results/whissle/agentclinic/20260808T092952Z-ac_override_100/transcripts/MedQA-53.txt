# MedQA-53  (MedQA, text mode)
correct diagnosis: Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV)

DOCTOR: Hello, I'm Dr. Agent, and I'll be looking into your symptoms today. Could you please describe exactly what you feel during these episodes of dizziness and vertigo?
PATIENT: It’s a very strange sensation, Doctor; everything starts spinning as if I'm on a merry-go-round. It usually happens suddenly, especially when I'm moving my head or changing positions.
DOCTOR: How long does each spinning sensation usually last, and have you noticed any changes in your hearing or ringing in your ears? (Question 2/20)
PATIENT: The spinning usually only lasts for a minute or less before it settles down. My hearing isn't what it used to be because of my age, but I haven't noticed any new changes or any ringing in my ears.
DOCTOR: Do you experience any nausea or vomiting when these spinning episodes occur? I would also like to perform a physical examination to check for certain movements in your eyes.

REQUEST TEST: Dix-Hallpike_Maneuver
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: Positive for right-sided horizontal nystagmus when moving from sitting to supine position, suggestive of BPPV
DOCTOR: Have you noticed any other symptoms like double vision, weakness in your limbs, or a severe headache when the spinning occurs? (Question 7/20)
PATIENT: No, I haven't had any headaches or trouble moving my arms and legs at all. My vision stays clear too, except for the world spinning around me for those few moments.
DOCTOR: That is good to hear that you aren't experiencing any neurological symptoms like weakness or vision changes. Aside from the brief spinning, do you feel completely normal and balanced between these episodes? (Question 5/20)
PATIENT: Yes, I feel perfectly fine once the spinning stops and I'm back to my usual self. I can go right back to my gardening or chores as long as I don't move my head too quickly.
DOCTOR: Have you had any recent head injuries or ear infections that might have preceded these symptoms? Also, are you currently taking any medications or do you have any other chronic medical conditions? (Question 6/20)
PATIENT: No, I haven't had any falls or ear infections lately, and my health has always been quite good. I'm not on any regular medications, and I don't have any chronic conditions to speak of.
DOCTOR: Dix-Hallpike Test: Positive right-sided horizontal nystagmus suggests right horizontal canal BPPV (though typically it's posterior canal, the test result specifically said horizontal).
PATIENT: Oh, that test certainly made my head spin and my eyes feel like they were dancing! Is that what you were looking for when you tilted me back like that?
DOCTOR: Yes, that eye movement helps confirm that tiny crystals in your inner ear have moved out of place. Have you ever experienced these specific spinning episodes before in your life? (Question 7/20)
PATIENT: No, this is the very first time I've ever felt anything like this. It’s quite a relief to hear there might be a simple explanation for it!
DOCTOR: It is very common for this to be a first-time occurrence, and it is indeed a manageable condition. Based on your symptoms and the test results, I have reached a conclusion.

DIAGNOS
PATIENT: That is wonderful news, Doctor, because I was starting to worry it might be something more serious. What exactly is the name for this, and is there a way to get those crystals back where they belong?
DOCTOR: It is called Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo, or BPPV, which is a common inner ear issue where small crystals move out of place. We can treat this with specific physical maneuvers to guide those crystals back into their proper position.

DIAGNOSIS READY: Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV)
