DOCTOR: Can you tell me how long you've been experiencing the shortness of breath and abdominal swelling, and whether it's been getting worse recently?
PATIENT: I've been noticing the shortness of breath for about a month now, and yes, it's definitely been getting worse. My belly has been getting bigger during that same time, which I thought was strange since I haven't changed my eating habits or anything.
DOCTOR: Have you noticed any weight loss, loss of appetite, or feeling full quickly when you eat, along with any pelvic pain or changes in your bowel or urinary habits?
PATIENT: I haven't really lost weight or had much change in my appetite, but now that you mention it, I do feel full more quickly than usual when I eat. I haven't had any pelvic pain though, and my bowel and urinary habits seem pretty normal.
DOCTOR: Have you gone through menopause, and is there any personal or family history of ovarian, breast, or colon cancer?
PATIENT: No, I haven't gone through menopause yet - my periods are still regular. And as far as I know, there's no history of ovarian, breast, or colon cancer in my family.
DOCTOR: I'd like to check for fluid buildup and better characterize the mass. REQUEST TEST: Pelvic_Ultrasound
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: The pelvic ultrasound was not specifically performed as a separate test, but an **Abdominal Ultrasound** was completed which includes visualization of the pelvic region.

**Abdominal Ultrasound Findings:**
- Large mass in the right ovary
- Significant ascites present

These findings are consistent with a right ovarian mass with associated fluid accumulation in the abdominal cavity.
DOCTOR: REQUEST TEST: CA-125_Blood_Test
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: CA-125 Level: Normal
DOCTOR: Given a normal CA-125 with a large ovarian mass and ascites, I'd like to rule out other causes — have you noticed any shortness of breath specifically when lying flat, or swelling in your legs?
PATIENT: Yes, actually the shortness of breath is definitely worse when I'm lying down at night - I've had to prop myself up with extra pillows to sleep. But no, I haven't noticed any swelling in my legs.
DOCTOR: REQUEST TEST: Chest_X-Ray
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: 

**Chest X-Ray Findings:**
- Right-sided pleural effusion

This indicates fluid accumulation in the pleural space (the area between the lung and chest wall) on the right side, which is consistent with your symptoms of shortness of breath, especially when lying down.
DOCTOR: This triad of ovarian mass, ascites, and pleural effusion (right-sided) with normal CA-125 strongly suggests Meigs' syndrome, a benign condition where a benign ovarian tumor (often fibroma) causes fluid buildup that resolves after tumor removal.

DIAGNOSIS READY: Meigs' Syndrome (benign ovarian fibroma with ascites and right pleural effusion) — recommend surgical resection of the ovarian mass for definitive diagnosis and resolution of symptoms.
