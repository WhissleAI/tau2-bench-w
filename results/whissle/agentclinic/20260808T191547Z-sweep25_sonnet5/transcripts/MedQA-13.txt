# MedQA-13  (MedQA, text mode)
correct diagnosis: Hirschsprung’s disease
outcome: correct

DOCTOR: Doctor: How old is the infant, and how long ago did the vomiting and inability to pass meconium begin?
PATIENT: He's only 2 days old, doctor. The vomiting started yesterday, the day after he was born, and he still hasn't had a bowel movement since birth.
DOCTOR: Doctor: Has the abdominal distension been getting worse, and does he have a tight, weak, or absent anal sphincter tone on exam?
PATIENT: Yes, his belly does seem to be getting more swollen and tight. I haven't really checked his bottom myself, doctor - I was hoping you could examine that.
DOCTOR: REQUEST TEST: Digital_Rectal_Exam
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS:

**Digital Rectal Exam:**
- **Anal Tone:** Tight anal sphincter
- **Rectal Vault:** Empty rectal vault with no stool present
- **Withdrawal:** Explosive discharge of gas and liquid stool upon finger withdrawal (positive "squirt sign")
- **Findings:** Consistent with functional obstruction; highly suggestive of Hirschsprung's disease given the clinical presentation
DOCTOR: That squirt sign strongly points to Hirschsprung's disease. I'd like to confirm with imaging — REQUEST TEST: Abdominal_X-Ray and Contrast_Enema
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS:

**Abdominal X-Ray:**
- **Findings:** Dilated loops of bowel with no gas in the rectum, suggestive of a distal obstruction.

**Contrast Enema:**
- **Findings:** The contrast study shows a transition zone in the rectosigmoid region with a narrowed distal segment and dilated proximal bowel loops, characteristic of Hirschsprung's disease. The contrast enema demonstrates the classic "cone-shaped" transition from the aganglionic narrow segment to the dilated ganglionic bowel.
DOCTOR: DIAGNOSIS READY: Hirschsprung's disease (congenital aganglionic megacolon) — confirmed by rectosigmoid transition zone on contrast enema and squirt sign on rectal exam; recommend rectal suction biopsy to confirm absence of ganglion cells and surgical consult.
