# MedQA-11  (MedQA, text mode)
correct diagnosis: Complex partial seizure
outcome: correct

DOCTOR: Can you tell me more about these episodes — how long do they last, and how often do they happen?
PATIENT: They last about 3 or 4 minutes each time. It's been happening pretty often this past month - maybe a few times, but I'm not really sure exactly how many because I don't remember them happening.
DOCTOR: Do you notice anything before an episode starts, like an unusual smell, taste, or feeling — an "aura"?
PATIENT: Yes, actually! Right before it happens, I get this weird muddy taste in my mouth. It's really strange and then I don't remember anything after that.
DOCTOR: Does anyone who's witnessed these episodes mention how long it takes you to become fully alert and act normal again afterward?
PATIENT: My brother said I seemed really tired and confused for a while after. Mom says it takes me maybe 10 or 15 minutes before I'm really back to normal and can answer questions properly.
DOCTOR: Do you ever bite your tongue, lose bladder control, or have jerking movements during these episodes, based on what witnesses have told you?
PATIENT: My brother said he saw me making some hand gestures during one of them, but I don't think anyone mentioned anything about biting my tongue or wetting myself.
DOCTOR: Doctor: Have you had any head injuries, family history of epilepsy or seizures, or been under unusual stress or sleep deprivation recently?
PATIENT: No, I haven't hit my head or anything like that. I don't think anyone in my family has had seizures before. I've been sleeping okay and school's been pretty normal - nothing really stressful going on.
DOCTOR: Doctor: Have you had an EEG or MRI before, and would you be open to getting one now so we can look at your brain activity more closely?
PATIENT: No, I've never had any tests like that done before. I guess that would be okay if it helps figure out what's going on with me.
DOCTOR: REQUEST TEST: EEG
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: Electroencephalogram (EEG) shows interictal spikes and sharp waves localized to the temporal lobes, more prominent on the right side.
DOCTOR: Doctor: That EEG finding fits with the aura, automatisms, and post-episode confusion you described — this looks like focal seizures arising from the temporal lobe. I'd like to get an MRI of your brain to check for a structural cause before we finalize things.

REQUEST TEST: MRI_Brain
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: MRI Brain shows no evidence of mass lesions, infarctions, or structural abnormalities.
DOCTOR: Doctor: Good news — no structural cause on MRI. Based on the aura, hand automatisms, confusion afterward, and EEG findings, this points to focal (temporal lobe) epilepsy, likely non-lesional.

DIAGNOSIS READY: Focal (temporal lobe) epilepsy with impaired awareness, non-lesional, presenting with gustatory aura, automatisms, and post-ictal confusion.
