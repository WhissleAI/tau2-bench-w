PATIENT: I'm not sure of the exact number for my CD4 count - I'd have to check my records. But yes, I am on antiretroviral therapy - I take efavirenz, tenofovir, and emtricitabine every day.
DOCTOR: Given painless vision loss with floaters in an AIDS patient, I'd like to check for CMV retinitis. REQUEST TEST: Dilated_Fundoscopic_Exam
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: Fundoscopy shows granular yellow-white opacities around the retinal vessels and multiple areas of dot-blot hemorrhages

These findings are consistent with CMV (cytomegalovirus) retinitis, which is an opportunistic infection that can occur in patients with severe immunosuppression (your CD4 count is 36/mm3, which is significantly below the normal range of 500-1500/mm3). The "pizza pie" appearance of yellow-white retinal opacities with hemorrhages is characteristic of this condition.
DOCTOR: DIAGNOSIS READY: CMV (Cytomegalovirus) Retinitis, given the classic fundoscopic "pizza-pie" appearance of granular yellow-white perivascular opacities with hemorrhages in a severely immunocompromised patient (CD4 36/mm³) presenting with painless floaters and vision loss. I recommend urgent ophthalmology referral for antiviral therapy (e.g., IV ganciclovir or valganciclovir) and close monitoring for retinal detachment.
