# MedQA-6  (MedQA, text mode)
correct diagnosis: Situational syncope
outcome: correct

DOCTOR: Can you tell me what happened during the episode—did anyone witness you lose consciousness, and do you recall feeling anything beforehand, like dizziness, palpitations, or nausea?
PATIENT: My wife found me on the bathroom floor after I passed out. I had just finished urinating when it happened. I don't remember feeling dizzy or having palpitations beforehand—it just happened suddenly.
DOCTOR: Did you feel warm, sweaty, or nauseated right before, or lose consciousness essentially right after standing up or straining—and how did you feel once you came to?
PATIENT: I didn't feel warm, sweaty, or nauseated beforehand. It happened right after I finished urinating, and when I came to, I felt completely fine—no confusion or anything like that.
DOCTOR: Have you had any similar fainting episodes in the past, particularly after urinating, coughing, or straining?
PATIENT: No, this has never happened to me before. This was the first time I've ever fainted like that.
DOCTOR: Have you had any recent illness, dehydration, or changes in medication—and do you have any history of heart problems or take medications for blood pressure?
PATIENT: No, I haven't been sick recently or dehydrated, and I'm not on any medications at all. I don't have any history of heart problems either—I've been pretty healthy overall.
DOCTOR: Have you noticed any injuries from the fall, like tongue biting, bruising, or loss of bladder/bowel control during the episode?
PATIENT: No, I didn't bite my tongue or have any bruising that I noticed. And I definitely didn't lose control of my bladder or bowels during the episode—everything was normal in that regard.
DOCTOR: Any family history of fainting, seizures, or sudden cardiac death at a young age?
PATIENT: No, there's no family history of fainting, seizures, or any sudden heart problems. Everyone in my family has been pretty healthy as far as I know.
DOCTOR: I'd like to get an EKG to check your heart rhythm and rule out any cardiac cause. REQUEST TEST: EKG
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: Electrocardiogram (EKG) shows no abnormalities. Normal sinus rhythm, normal rate (72 bpm), no evidence of arrhythmias, conduction delays, or ischemic changes.
DOCTOR: Good, that's reassuring. Just to check—do you recall your age, and have you had any recent large meals or been standing for a long time before this happened?
PATIENT: I'm 53 years old. No, I hadn't eaten a large meal recently, and I wasn't standing for a long time—I was just at home, went to the bathroom, and it happened right after I finished urinating.
DOCTOR: This sounds like classic micturition syncope—a vasovagal reflex triggered by urination. Given your normal exam and EKG, it's benign, but I'd advise avoiding standing too quickly after urinating and sitting down if you feel any warning signs.

DIAGNOSIS READY: Micturition (situational) syncope
