# MedQA-12  (MedQA, text mode)
correct diagnosis: Posterior hip dislocation
outcome: correct

DOCTOR: Hi, I'm Dr. Agent. Can you tell me what happened and how your right hip and leg are feeling right now?
PATIENT: I was in a car accident - someone hit me from behind while I was stopped. My right groin area is killing me, it's really severe pain, and both my knees hurt too. My right leg just feels... wrong, like I can't position it normally.
DOCTOR: Can you describe how your right leg looks right now — is it shortened, rotated inward or outward, and can you move it at all?
PATIENT: Yes, my right leg looks shorter than my left one, and it's kind of bent and turned inward. I can barely move it at all - any attempt to move it makes the groin pain so much worse.
DOCTOR: This sounds like a posterior hip dislocation. Given the mechanism—dashboard-type injury in a collision with knee trauma—I'd like to check your knees too: do you have pain, swelling, or deformity there, or any numbness/tingling down the leg?
PATIENT: Yes, both my knees are painful, especially when I try to bend them, but I don't think they're swollen or look deformed. I don't have any numbness or tingling down my leg though, just that terrible pain in my groin.
DOCTOR: Any numbness or weakness in your foot, or can you still move your toes and feel your foot normally?
PATIENT: I can move my toes and feel my foot okay - no numbness or weakness there. It's really just the groin pain and not being able to move my hip that's the problem.
DOCTOR: I'd like to get imaging to confirm — REQUEST TEST: Pelvis_and_Hip_X-Ray
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: Pelvic X-ray shows no fractures identified in the pelvic ring or femoral neck, but alignment suggests posterior dislocation of the right hip.
DOCTOR: Given the dashboard mechanism, I also want to check your knees for any occult injury — REQUEST TEST: Knee_X-Ray_Bilateral
MEASUREMENT: RESULTS: Knee X-rays are not available in the provided data.

NORMAL READINGS - No fractures or dislocations identified in bilateral knee imaging. Patellofemoral joints appear intact with normal alignment.
DOCTOR: Good, no fractures in the knees. Given the confirmed posterior hip dislocation with intact neurovascular status, this needs urgent closed reduction — I'll arrange immediate orthopedic consultation.

DIAGNOSIS READY: Right posterior hip dislocation (following motor vehicle collision, dashboard-type injury), with associated bilateral knee contusions and intact distal neurovascular function.
